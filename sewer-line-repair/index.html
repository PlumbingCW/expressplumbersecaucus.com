Repair or clean: how to tell

A line needs repair, not another cleaning, when the same blockage returns, when a camera shows damage, or when sewage backs up with no clog found. These are the signs:

Repeat backups

Cleared twice in a year, blocked again.

Sinkholes or soggy lawn

Soil washing into a broken pipe or sewage leaking out.

Sewer smell outside

Gas escaping from a crack or open joint.

Slow drains after a clear

A belly or offset that traps solids.

When a sewer line failure is an emergency

A full stoppage with sewage in the house is urgent. A suspected collapse is urgent too, because the line cannot be cleared at all. Do these while you wait:

  • Stop every water source in the house.
  • Do not flush or run the washer.
  • Keep children and pets out of wet areas and wash hands after any contact.
  • Note where the water appears, since that helps us find the break.
  • Call the 24/7 line.

Repair options and the honest trade-offs

Open-cut repair digs the yard or floor, removes the damaged pipe and installs new pipe. It is the most certain fix and the most disruptive. Trenchless lining pulls a resin-saturated liner through the old pipe and cures it in place, which avoids most digging but works only if the pipe is mostly intact and the path is clear. Pipe bursting pulls new pipe through the old path and suits long runs with few bends.

Permits and who decides

Sewer repairs inside a town usually need a permit, and permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department before work starts. The department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue. Sewer discharge rules in town come from the Secaucus Municipal Utilities Authority, whose rules the town code recognizes as the town sewer rules. We tell you which approvals your repair is likely to need, and the contractor on the job handles the paperwork they are responsible for.

Why Secaucus sewer lines need a careful look

Secaucus is an island in the Meadowlands, with the Hackensack River on its west side and Penhorn and Cromakill Creeks on its east. Wet, soft ground moves, and a lateral that settles can develop a belly. That is a practical reason to camera a line before spending on a repair, and again after if the pipe was bedded in wet soil.

On September 1, 2021, Ida flooded parts of town and the town pumped out 91 basements. A line that was marginal before heavy rain can fail after it, so a backup that follows a storm is worth a camera look.

Call 811 before any digging

Digging for a sewer repair in a yard or a driveway can hit gas, electric and communication lines. In New Jersey, whoever is excavating must contact New Jersey One Call at 811 at least three business days before outdoor digging. Trenchless lining avoids most of that digging, which is one reason it is worth pricing. The repair itself follows the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.
