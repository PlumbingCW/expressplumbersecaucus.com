Toilet Installation Cost in Secaucus, NJ: What Sets the Price

The price of a toilet installation in Secaucus comes down to five things: the toilet you choose, the condition of the flange and floor, the shutoff valve and supply line, how easy the bathroom is to reach, and the time of the call. A straight swap of a sound toilet is the low end. A rotted floor or a broken flange is where it climbs.

A firm number needs a short conversation, because every bathroom is different. This guide shows what changes the price so you can compare quotes fairly.

What a proper installation includes

A good job is more than setting a bowl.

  • Shutting off water and draining the old toilet.
  • Removing the old toilet and the old wax ring.
  • Checking the flange for cracks and level.
  • Setting the new toilet on a new wax ring or gasket.
  • Tightening the bolts evenly so the china does not crack.
  • Connecting a new supply line and checking the shutoff valve.
  • Testing the flush and looking for leaks at the base.

Flanges and floors: the hidden cost

The flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet seats on. If it is cracked, rusted or too low, the toilet rocks or leaks. Repair ranges from a repair ring to replacing a section of pipe. Water at the base for a long time can rot the subfloor. We look before quoting and tell you what we find. If the toilet is leaking now, see toilet repair.

Choosing a toilet that fits

Measure the rough-in, which is the distance from the wall to the center of the drain. Twelve inches is common, but older homes vary. A toilet with the wrong rough-in will not fit. Bowl shape and height also matter. An elongated bowl is roomier, and comfort height suits many adults.

Doing it yourself versus a plumber

A swap of a sound toilet is a manageable DIY job for a handy person. The risks are cracking the china, a bad seal that leaks into the floor, and discovering a damaged flange midway. If you have an upstairs bathroom, a condo below you or an unfamiliar flange, a plumber is the safer path.

When a toilet job turns into something else

If a new toilet backs up, or the water in the base rises when another fixture drains, the toilet was not the problem. See drain cleaning for clogs that involve more than one fixture.

How the parts seal

The toilet bowl seals to the flange with a wax ring or a rubber gasket. The tank bolts to the bowl with a gasket between them, and the supply line connects through a shutoff valve. Each seal is a place for a leak if it is rushed. Over-tightening bolts cracks the china, and under-tightening lets the toilet rock and break the wax seal.

Flush ratings and water use

Toilets made today use far less water than older ones. Federal rules cap new toilets at 1.6 gallons per flush, and models labeled WaterSense use 1.28 gallons or less. A modern unit saves water every day, which can show up on your bill. A toilet from before the 1990s used much more, so the swap often pays back over time.

Bidet seats and outlets

An electric bidet seat needs a nearby outlet, and a bathroom outlet must be protected by a ground-fault device. Water supply for the seat needs a T fitting on the shutoff. If there is no outlet, an electrician adds one, and that cost is separate from the plumbing.

What to ask in a quote

Compare quotes line by line.

  • Is the toilet included or supplied by me?
  • Does the price include a new wax ring, bolts and supply line?
  • What happens if the flange or floor is damaged?
  • Is haul-away of the old toilet included?
  • Is there a different rate for evenings or weekends?

Toilet work in Secaucus condos and townhouses

Many Secaucus residents live in stacked buildings, where a leak at the base of a toilet can stain the ceiling below. Ask your management office about rules for contractors and whether you must notify them before a swap. Permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department whether a toilet replacement at your address needs one. The department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue, and its hours are listed on the town website.

Neighbors in Jersey City and Lyndhurst face the same stacked-building questions, and we travel there from Secaucus too.
