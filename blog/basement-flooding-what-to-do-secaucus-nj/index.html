Basement Flooding in Secaucus, NJ: First Steps and Fixes

If your basement is flooding, the first steps are to stay safe, stop adding water, and work out where the water is coming from. Stay out of standing water if the panel or outlets are in the basement. Stop running water upstairs, and note whether the water is clear, dirty or smells of sewage, because that tells you the source.

This guide covers the first hour, how to tell the common causes apart, and what to do afterward. Our line is open 24/7 if you need a crew now.

The first hour

Do these in order.

  1. Safety

    Do not wade near the panel or outlets. If water is rising toward them, stay out and call the utility or an electrician.

  2. Stop water use

    No flushing, laundry or dishwasher, in case the sewer is full.

  3. Look at the water

    Clear, dirty or sewage-smelling water points to different sources.

  4. Lift and document

    Move what you can above the water line and photograph everything.

  5. Call

    Call a plumber for pump or sewer trouble, and your insurer to report.

Three causes, three answers

Basement water usually comes from groundwater, a sewer backup or a plumbing leak, and the fix differs.

Telling basement water sources apart
SourceCluesFirst move
GroundwaterSeeps at the wall or floor joint, clear water, after rainCheck sump pump, then drainage
Sewer backupRises from the floor drain, dirty, smellsStop water use, call for a cable or jet
Plumbing leakNear a pipe or heater, meter movesShut the main, call for leak detection

If it is the sump pump

A pump that is not running is the most fixable cause. Check the plug and the breaker, and lift the float. A silent pump needs repair or replacement, and a battery backup protects against a power cut during a storm. See sump pump repair for the options.

If it is a sewer backup

Water coming up the floor drain means the main is blocked or overloaded. Do not use water and call. A cable or a jet clears a blockage, and a camera shows whether the line is damaged. See sewer line cleaning. If the backup follows heavy rain, the sewer may be overloaded, and no plumber can fix that from inside the house.

After the water is gone

Dry the space fast. Wet drywall and carpet grow mold within a couple of days, so pull wet carpet and open wet walls. Throw away porous items that touched sewage water. Keep photos, a list of damaged items and receipts. For chronic seepage, see basement waterproofing.

Insurance and what it usually covers

Policies differ, and flood, sewer backup and groundwater seepage are often covered separately from a burst pipe. Call your agent early and ask exactly what is included. Do not assume. A plumber's written description of the cause helps your claim.

Mold timelines and drying

Mold can start growing within one to two days in damp materials. Fans and dehumidifiers help once standing water is gone. Wet drywall that stays wet should be removed and not painted over. If the water was sewage, porous materials such as carpet and insulation are discarded rather than dried.

Cleaning up after sewer water

Sewage water carries bacteria. Wear gloves and boots, avoid touching your face and wash hands after. Hard surfaces can be cleaned and disinfected. Do not run a household vacuum on wet floors, and do not mix cleaning chemicals. If you are unsure, a restoration contractor is the right call.

Prevention checklist

These steps reduce the odds of a repeat.

  • Test the sump pump and float each season.
  • Install a battery or water-powered backup for the pump.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts discharging away from the foundation.
  • Ask a plumber whether a backwater valve suits your sewer lateral.
  • Raise stored items off the floor.
  • Know where your main water shutoff is.

Who to call

A plumber handles pumps, sewer and leaks. An electrician checks wet panels and outlets. A restoration company handles drying and mold. Your insurer opens a claim. Call each early, and keep a log of times and names. For help from us, our line is open 24/7.

Basement flooding in Secaucus: what happened in 2021

On September 1, 2021, during the remnants of Ida, parts of Secaucus flooded. Local reports named Centre Avenue, Farm Road, Valley Court and Harmon Cove, where carports had about a foot of floodwater. The town pumped out 91 basements, and more than 100 homes had flooding. The town's own Ida recovery notice, dated October 29, 2021, included a Basement Flooding Fact Sheet from FEMA material.

Secaucus is an island in the Meadowlands between the Hackensack River and creeks, and low ground fills fast. After a flood, floodplain rules for substantially damaged structures can apply, so ask the Secaucus construction department before major repairs. It is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue.

Low ground is not unique to Secaucus. Lyndhurst sits beside the Meadowlands wetlands and Rutherford borders the Hackensack River, so the same first-hour steps apply.
