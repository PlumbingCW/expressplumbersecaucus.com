Water Heater Igniter Replacement in Secaucus, NJ Homes

A water heater igniter that has failed leaves a gas tank with no heat: the control calls for flame, the igniter does not glow or spark, and the burner never lights. Replacing the igniter is a modest repair on a healthy tank, and it usually takes under an hour once the right part is in hand.

Before you spend money, it helps to know how your heater lights, how to tell an igniter fault from other causes, and when a part is not worth buying.

Standing pilot or electronic ignition

Older gas heaters use a standing pilot, a small flame that stays lit and a thermocouple that proves it. Newer ones use an electronic ignition, either a spark igniter or a hot surface igniter that glows to light the burner. The parts and the faults differ, so identify your type first by looking at the control and the manual.

Signs the igniter has failed

Look for these patterns:

  • No glow or no spark when the heater calls for heat.
  • Clicking sounds but no flame.
  • An error code on the control that points to ignition.
  • The pilot lights but the main burner will not start.
  • Intermittent no-heat that clears for a day, then returns.

Other faults that look the same

A dirty flame sensor, a failed gas valve, a blocked vent or air intake, and low gas pressure can all stop a burner. Replacing an igniter that was never the problem wastes a visit. A diagnosis checks voltage, flame rectification and the vent before ordering parts. See water heater repair for the usual faults.

Replacing a hot surface igniter

With power and gas off, the burner assembly is accessed and the old igniter is unplugged and removed. The new igniter is fragile and should not be touched on the heating element. It is installed, the assembly is restored, and the unit is lit and watched through a full cycle. This is work for a qualified person, because it involves gas and combustion.

Repair or replace the whole heater

An igniter is a small part. If the tank is leaking, rusted at the base or more than about a dozen years old, spending money on parts may not be the best use of it. If the tank is sound and the igniter is the only failure, repair makes sense. If the tank is failing, see replacement.

Safety

If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911. Do not try to light a heater that smells of gas. Never bypass a safety device. We give no response-time promise for gas leaks.

How a technician tests the igniter

With power off, the igniter is checked for continuity and resistance and compared with the manufacturer's range. A hot surface igniter that fails the test is replaced. One that passes may still fail under load, which is why a technician also checks the voltage that reaches it and the control board that supplies it. Testing is part of why a diagnosis comes before a part.

Getting the right part

Igniters are model-specific. The model and serial number on the data plate identify the unit and the correct part. A part that looks similar can have a different resistance and cause nuisance lockouts. Photograph the data plate before you call.

The flame sensor

A flame sensor is a thin metal rod that proves the burner is lit. When it is coated with soot, the control shuts the burner off after a few seconds even though the igniter worked. Cleaning it with fine abrasive cloth is a common fix, and it often looks like an igniter problem from the outside.

Cold weather and combustion air

A heater in a cold garage or a sealed closet may struggle in winter. Cold air, wind at the vent and a blocked air intake each cause lockouts. If your heater fails on the coldest mornings, tell the technician.

Habits that extend the life of the unit

Keep the area around the heater clear of lint and storage, and flush the tank occasionally. A clean burner chamber and clear combustion air reduce stress on the igniter and the control board.

Safety limits for a do-it-yourself repair

An igniter sits beside the burner and the gas valve. If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility from outside. Do not relight a heater that repeatedly fails to ignite, and never bypass a safety device. A pilot or igniter that fails often points to a dirty burner or a weak thermocouple, and that diagnosis is quick for a plumber with the right meter.

Igniter problems in Secaucus homes

Condo closets and utility rooms in Secaucus can restrict airflow around a gas heater, and poor airflow is a common reason a burner will not stay lit. Dust and lint also build up in older units, and a good cleaning sometimes clears a no-heat. Permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department whether gas appliance work at your address needs a permit. The department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue.

The same diagnosis applies in Lyndhurst and Rutherford, where older basements often hold the heater.

Rules and limits on igniter work

Gas-fired appliance work in New Jersey follows the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. A dead igniter is a simple part, but the gas valve and burner around it are not, and a gas smell means leave the home and call your gas utility from outside.
