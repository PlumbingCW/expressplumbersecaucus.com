Basement Waterproofing in Secaucus, NJ

Basement waterproofing in Secaucus is about finding where water comes in and choosing the right fix: interior drainage, a sump pump, exterior grading, wall sealing, or sewer work. The wrong fix wastes money, so the first step is working out the source.

Interior drainage and sump systems

An interior drain channel along the footing collects water that reaches the wall and carries it to a sump pit, where a pump discharges it outside. It is the most common plumbing-side fix for chronic seepage. The pump needs a backup, which our sump pump page describes.

Wall sealing and its limits

Sealers and coatings can help with dampness, but they do not stop water under pressure. We say clearly when a coating is enough and when it is not, and we do not sell a coating for a drain or sewer problem.

What to check before calling

A few observations cut the guesswork.

  • Does the water appear only after heavy rain, or also on dry days?
  • Is it at the floor drain, along the wall or at the joint where the wall meets the floor?
  • Do the gutters discharge near the foundation?
  • Does the water meter move when everything is off?
  • Is there a sewer smell?

Start outside: gutters, grading and window wells

The cheapest fix is often outside. Downspouts that dump beside the foundation, soil that slopes toward the wall, and window wells that fill with rain put water exactly where it can get in. A common rule of thumb is soil falling about six inches over the first ten feet away from the house, and downspouts that discharge several feet out.

We tell you when outside work should come first, since it can stop a problem for far less than an interior system. We do not do landscaping, so we point out what to ask a landscaper or mason to do, and we handle the plumbing side: sump discharge, drain lines and backflow.

What a waterproofing visit looks like

Basements in Secaucus

Secaucus is an island in the Meadowlands, with the Hackensack River on one side and creeks on the other, so the water table is shallow in places.

During Ida on September 1, 2021, the town pumped out 91 basements, and more than 100 homes had flooding. The town's Ida recovery notice included a Basement Flooding Fact Sheet. In low areas near Centre Avenue and Farm Road, flood-prone sites may be affected by floodplain rules, so ask the construction department before major work. The department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue.

Waterproofing cannot stop a river flood. It manages seepage and groundwater. For flood events, protection means a working pump, a backup and careful storage.

Digging outside the foundation

An exterior drain or a new discharge line means outdoor digging. In New Jersey, whoever is excavating must contact New Jersey One Call at 811 at least three business days ahead. Drain and discharge piping follows the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and Secaucus permit rules vary, so ask the construction department.
