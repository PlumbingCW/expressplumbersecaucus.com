Jam, hum or leak: what each one means

The sound and the leak location tell us most of it.

Humming, no spin

A jammed grinding plate. A hex wrench in the bottom socket and the reset button often free it.

Dead silent

Tripped reset, a switch or a failed motor.

Leak from the bottom

Failed seal or cracked housing. Usually replacement.

Leak at the sink flange or drain

Loose or worn mount or gasket. Often repairable.

When a disposal problem is an emergency

Call at once if:

  • Water is pooling under the sink or running down the cabinet.
  • The sink is backed up and wastewater is rising.
  • The unit is smoking, smells of burning or trips the breaker.
  • A fork or spoon has fallen in and the unit is running.

Turn the disposal's wall switch and the circuit off. If the sink drains slowly even with no disposal use, the clog may be in the line. See drain cleaning.

Repair or replace

A disposal lasts around ten years on average. If it is newer and the problem is a jam, a reset or a gasket, repair costs less than replacing. If the body leaks, the motor has failed or the unit is far past its age, replacement is the better value. We install units sized to the household, from basic to heavier-duty models.

What not to put down it

Fibrous vegetables like celery and corn husks wrap around the shredder. Grease coats the pipe beyond. Pasta and rice swell. Coffee grounds pack. Running cold water for fifteen seconds after use moves waste past the first bend, where kitchen lines typically clog.

What a new disposal installation involves

Installation is a short job when the sink and electrical connection are ready.

  1. Disconnect

    Power off, the old unit and mount removed.

  2. Mount

    New mounting ring and sink flange sealed.

  3. Connect

    Dishwasher inlet, drain trap and power.

  4. Test

    Run water, check for leaks, test the dishwasher drain.

A dishwasher drain that connects to the disposal needs the knockout plug removed or the dishwasher will not drain. It is a common first-time mistake, and we check it.

Disposals in Secaucus apartments and townhouses

Some buildings restrict disposals, or limit them to certain units, so check your lease or association rules before installing one. In stacked buildings, kitchen lines share a stack and a grease clog can back up a unit below.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department whether an installation needs one. The department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue.

Rules behind a disposal job

Disposal drains and traps follow the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and the electrical connection falls under the state's electrical rules. Some condo buildings ban disposals, so check your association's rules first.
