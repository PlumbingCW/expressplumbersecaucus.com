Emergency Toilet Repair and Installation in Secaucus, NJ

Emergency toilet repair in Secaucus covers a toilet that overflows, leaks at the base, will not stop running or will not flush. Dispatch is open 24/7. If it is overflowing, turn the shutoff valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise, lift the tank lid and hold the flapper closed, and call (551) 249-3045.

A running toilet that only wastes water can wait for a scheduled visit. A leak at the base can rot the subfloor, so do not wait on that one.

When a toilet problem is an emergency

These need attention now:

  • Water overflowing the bowl onto the floor.
  • A toilet that bubbles when another fixture drains. That points to the main line.
  • Water seeping around the base or from the supply line.
  • A crack in the bowl or tank that leaks.
  • A toilet that will not stop filling and is flooding the floor.

If other drains in the house are slow, the problem is not the toilet. See sewer line cleaning.

A running toilet and your water bill

A toilet that runs quietly can waste a large amount of water over a month. To check, put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing, and look at the bowl. If color appears, the flapper leaks. It is the most common source of a mystery rise in the water bill, and the next step is the leak detection page if the toilet is fine.

Toilets in Secaucus homes and buildings

In stacked buildings, a leaking toilet can damage the ceiling of the unit below, so a slow weep at the base is worth fixing quickly. Tell your management office if you see staining in a ceiling after a neighbor's toilet use.

If a toilet backs up during heavy rain, the cause may be the sewer system filling. On September 1, 2021, during Ida, the town pumped out 91 basements. Tell the dispatcher when the problem started so we can tell stormwater from a clog.

Flush volume and what a replacement means

Federal rules cap new toilets at 1.6 gallons per flush, and WaterSense-labeled models use 1.28 gallons or less. A toilet from before the 1990s may use far more, so repeated repairs on an old one can cost more than a replacement. The installation follows the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.
