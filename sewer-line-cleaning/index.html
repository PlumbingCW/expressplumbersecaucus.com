Sewer backup emergency: the first ten minutes

A main line stoppage is a household-wide event, not a sink problem. The first minutes decide how much floor you have to clean.

  • Turn off the washing machine and stop all showers and sinks.
  • Do not run the dishwasher or flush any toilet.
  • Switch off power to any basement area with water reaching outlets, only if you can reach the panel dry.
  • Open a window if there is a strong sewer smell.
  • Call and tell us whether the cleanout is inside or outside.

Signs the main line is the problem

A single slow sink is a branch problem. These patterns point to the main:

Gurgling toilet

Air pushed back through the traps when other fixtures drain.

Floor drain backing up

The lowest opening in the house shows the blockage first.

Multiple floors slow

Fixtures on different levels share only the main.

Recurring odor

Sewer gas rising from a dry or backed-up trap.

How a main line gets cleaned

We locate the cleanout, which is usually near the foundation, in the basement, or at the property line. A drum cable machine with a cutting head goes in first and breaks up the blockage. We then flush the line and run a camera when the cause is unclear.

If grease or scale is the cause, hydro jetting finishes the job. If the camera finds a break, a bellied section or heavy root growth, we show it to you and talk through repair or lining before anything else.

Cause by cause

Most main line stoppages fit one of four causes. Roots enter through joints in older clay or cast iron. Grease cools and builds up from kitchen drains. Wipes and similar items that are labeled flushable catch on rough pipe. And a low spot in the pipe holds water and solids until they form a plug. Each needs a different follow-up, which is why a camera look after the cable is worth the few extra minutes.

Where the line ends

Sewer lines are covered by the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15 inside the property, and the town's sewer rules apply beyond the property line. In Secaucus, the Secaucus Municipal Utilities Authority sets those rules. If a camera shows the blockage past your cleanout, tell us, because the public side may be someone else's job.
