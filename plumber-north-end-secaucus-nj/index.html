Plumber in the North End, Secaucus, NJ

The North End is the part of Secaucus north of Route 3. Local references describe it as one of the older, traditional residential neighborhoods of town, with Secaucus High School, Schmidt's Woods and Mill Creek Marsh nearby. Paterson Plank Road connects it to the Plaza, the town's downtown area.

Most homes here are houses, so the plumbing questions are usually about the water heater, the drains, the sewer line and basement water. To ask about your own address, call (551) 249-3045.

Where the North End sits

The town gives the North End no formal boundary that we could verify, so we describe it by its anchors. It lies north of Route 3, borders the marsh and woods on its edge and reaches the Plaza by way of Paterson Plank Road. If you are unsure whether your street counts, the practical test is simple: tell us your address and we handle it the same way.

Why an older neighborhood does not mean old pipes

Sources call the North End one of the older residential areas, but none of them give the age of its houses or the pipe materials used. We do not assume that any particular house has galvanized steel, cast iron or clay sewer pipe. A house of any age may have been repiped, relined or renovated. The reliable way to know is to look, and for drains and sewer lines the look is a camera.

A camera before a guess

A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe material, the joints and any damage. It is useful before a purchase or before digging.

Marsh edges and basements

Secaucus is in the Meadowlands, and Mill Creek Marsh is part of the North End's surroundings. Low ground and marsh edges make groundwater and storm runoff worth thinking about for any basement, though we have no evidence that a specific North End street floods. On September 1, 2021, the remnants of Ida flooded Centre Avenue, Farm Road and Valley Court, and the town pumped out 91 basements. The town's own Ida notice that October included a Basement Flooding Fact Sheet.

Before you decide whether water in a basement is a plumbing problem, check the meter. Shut every fixture, read the meter, wait, and read it again. A moving dial means a plumbing leak. A still dial after rain points to groundwater or a drain, covered in our basement waterproofing page.

A homeowner's checklist

A few checks help any house, whatever its age.

  • Find the main water shutoff and make sure it turns.
  • Note where the sewer cleanout is, if there is one.
  • Look at the water heater's data plate for its age.
  • Check under sinks and behind toilets for slow drips.
  • Keep a record of past plumbing work and who did it.

Permits and the town

Permit rules vary, so ask before work starts. The Secaucus construction department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue, (201) 330-2027, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Sewer rules in Secaucus come from the Secaucus Municipal Utilities Authority, so ask it where your responsibility for the lateral ends.
