Water heater emergency: what to do first when it leaks

A tank that leaks from the body cannot be patched. A leak from a fitting or relief valve may be repairable, so shutting things off and looking matters.

  • Turn off the gas control or flip the breaker so the unit stops heating.
  • Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the tank.
  • Open a hot tap elsewhere in the house to relieve pressure.
  • Move anything stored near the heater and mop standing water.
  • Take a photo of the data plate and the leak for your insurer.

Repair or replace: a simple test

Age and leak location decide this. Tank heaters commonly run out of life somewhere around ten to fifteen years, depending on use and water conditions.

Choosing the right replacement

Size, fuel and venting are the three decisions. A household of one or two people usually does well with a 40-gallon tank, three to four with 50, and larger families or heavy use may want more. Keep the same fuel unless you are ready to run new gas or electric lines. Venting matters: a unit that vents through a chimney has different requirements from one that vents out a wall.

If you want to compare a tank with a tankless unit, we explain the plumbing and gas changes before you decide. Planned work with more choices is on the installation page.

What a replacement visit includes

We aim for one visit.

  1. Shut down and drain

    Gas or power off, water off, tank drained safely.

  2. Remove

    The old unit is disconnected and hauled away.

  3. Set and connect

    New tank, flex or hard lines, shutoff valve, relief valve and discharge pipe.

  4. Vent and test

    Gas lines checked for leaks, venting confirmed, temperature set.

We also look at the pan, the expansion tank and the shutoff valves, since those older parts often fail soon after a new heater goes in.
