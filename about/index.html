About Secaucus Express Plumbing

Secaucus Express Plumbing is a local emergency plumbing service at 700 Plaza Drive, Secaucus, NJ. We answer one line, 24/7, and send a licensed and insured plumber to Secaucus, North Bergen, Lyndhurst, Jersey City and Rutherford.

Call (551) 249-3045

How we work

The call comes first. You describe the problem and the address, and we tell you what to shut off while the crew is on the way. On site, you hear a clear price before work starts, and we explain what failed so it does not happen again.

We handle drains, sewer lines, water heaters, leaks, burst pipes, sump pumps and planned installs. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

What we believe about plumbing advice

A plumber should say what is wrong in plain words, and should say when a job is not worth doing. We write our guides the same way. They cover what you can try, what to avoid and when to call.

In New Jersey, plumbing work is done by a licensed master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or their employees. The state Division of Consumer Affairs explains the rules on its website. Ask any contractor to show you what they hold before work starts.

Where we work

Secaucus is an island in the Meadowlands, with the Hackensack River on its west side. That low ground shapes much of what we see: floor drain backups after heavy rain, sump pumps that run all spring and condo risers that serve several units. Our guides and service pages reflect those local conditions. See drain cleaning and basement waterproofing for examples.

Our towns: North Bergen, Lyndhurst, Jersey City and Rutherford.

What a call looks like

A dispatcher answers and asks four things: the address, what you see, whether water is still running and whether the building is a house, a condo or an apartment. The last answer changes the plan, because condos and apartments have shared risers and building rules.

While the crew heads your way, we tell you which valve to close, what to keep dry and what to leave alone. If the job is not an emergency, we offer the next same-day appointment when one is available, and tell you plainly when the next opening is later.

What we handle

Our emergency work covers drain cleaning, burst pipe repair, leak detection, sewer line repair and sump pump repair. Planned work covers water heater installation, tankless units, repiping and basement waterproofing. The full list is on the services page.

What we do not do

We do not promise a fixed arrival time, because traffic on Route 3 or the Turnpike and the number of calls in the queue change it. We tell you what we expect when you call. We do not guess at a price over the phone for a problem we have not seen, because the real cause often differs from the symptom.

We also do not tell you a repair is needed when a simple fix will do. A jammed disposal, a tripped reset or a loose connection are things we explain, and sometimes you can handle them yourself.

Local knowledge we use

Secaucus has about 9,316 housing units and a mix of condos, townhouses and older homes. Harmon Cove was developed in the 1970s, and its buildings share risers and stacks. On September 1, 2021, the remnants of Ida flooded Centre Avenue, Farm Road, Valley Court and Harmon Cove, and the town pumped out 91 basements. That is why our pages on sump pumps and drains talk about storms and groundwater.

Permit rules vary by town and by job. The Secaucus construction department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue, (201) 330-2027, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., and we tell you what to ask before work starts.

How we write our guides

Our blog posts answer one question each, in plain words, and say what to check where you live. We name the Secaucus office that handles permits, the utility that handles sewer rules and the facts of local storms, and we leave out numbers we cannot back up. For example, published figures for local water hardness disagree with each other, so we do not state one.

When a guide mentions a cost, it explains what drives the price instead of quoting a number that would be wrong next year. When a topic is outside plumbing, such as electrical service for a tankless heater, we say which licensed trade handles it.

If you spot something in a guide that does not match what you see at home, call (551) 249-3045 and tell us. A good correction makes the page better for the next reader.

Codes and standards we cite

Plumbing in New Jersey follows the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. The local construction office enforces it, and in Secaucus that is the construction department at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue. We name the code so you can check our advice. It describes the rules we work to, and it is not a certification we hold.

For digging, New Jersey law points to New Jersey One Call, which you reach by dialing 811. For lead service lines, New Jersey's 2021 law, P.L. 2021, c.183, sets a ten-year replacement deadline for public water systems.
