A 1920s house and its plumbing

Most of Rutherford's current housing was built during the 1920s, and homes of that age commonly have galvanized supply lines, cast iron drain stacks and clay or cast iron laterals. Each fails differently. Galvanized pipe corrodes from inside and loses pressure. Cast iron scales inside and cracks. Clay laterals take root growth at the joints.

That is why Rutherford calls often lead to a camera and a plan. See sewer camera inspection and repiping for what each involves.

Rutherford details

Rutherford is in Bergen County, about 18,800 residents in 2020, bounded by the Passaic River on the west and the Hackensack River on the southeast, where it borders Secaucus. Route 17 skirts its southeast edge and Route 3 runs along its southwest. The borough's train station, built by the Erie Railroad in 1898, is the last stop before Secaucus Junction.

The borough's building department is at 176 Park Avenue and can be reached at (201) 460-3000. Its permit page says a building permit may include plumbing permits, and it does not say whether a water heater swap needs one, so call to confirm for your job. The borough's website posts notices when Veolia flushes water mains, which can stir up sediment in your pipes.
