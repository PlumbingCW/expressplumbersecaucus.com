Emergency plumbing in Secaucus: what makes this town different

Secaucus is an island in the Meadowlands. The Hackensack River runs along the west side, and Penhorn and Cromakill Creeks run to the east. Low ground and a shallow water table mean that heavy rain finds basements and floor drains first. On September 1, 2021, the remnants of Ida flooded Centre Avenue, Farm Road, Valley Court and Harmon Cove, and the town pumped out 91 basements.

If a drain backs up in a storm, close the fixtures and call the 24/7 line. A backup on a dry day is usually a clog, and a backup in a storm usually points at drainage, so tell us which it was.

The town has about 9,316 housing units, and many are condos and townhouses. Harmon Cove was built in the 1970s, and its buildings share risers and stacks, so a leak in one unit can affect others. Tell us the floor and the building when you call. We plan the shutoff around the building before we arrive.

The business side of town matters too. Secaucus Junction, Mill Creek Mall and the Harmon Meadow offices sit near Route 3, the New Jersey Turnpike and Meadowlands Parkway. Our dispatch point at 700 Plaza Drive is close to all of them, so we reach the Plaza Drive office corridor, County Avenue and Paterson Plank Road quickly. For a restaurant, an office suite or a retail unit, see commercial plumbing.

What to do first

Before the plumber arrives

Close the mainTurn the main valve clockwise until it stops. In a building, close the unit valve first.
Stop using waterDo not run taps or flush until we say it is safe. Standing water in a drain can rise again.
Protect the floorMove rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area.
Stay clear of powerDo not touch outlets or appliances in standing water. Switch off breakers only if the panel is dry.
Skip chemical openersThey burn skin and make a clog harder to clear safely.
Take photosPictures of the damage help with an insurance claim.
