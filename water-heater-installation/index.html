How to size a new water heater

Capacity depends on how many people use hot water at the same time, not only how many live in the home.

Typical tank sizes by household
HouseholdTypical tank
1 to 2 people30 to 40 gallons
3 to 4 people40 to 50 gallons
5 or more, or heavy use50 to 80 gallons

These are starting points. A home with a soaking tub or a second full bath at peak hours may want the next size up. We confirm after a short conversation, not by guess.

Fuel, venting and space

Three site conditions decide what can go where.

Gas

Needs a gas line, a shutoff and a vent. Chimney and direct-vent units differ.

Electric

Needs a dedicated circuit of the right size and a place to drain.

Closet or condo

Needs access, a drain pan and often building approval.

Basement

Needs a path for removal and a floor drain or pan.

What a complete installation includes

A good installation includes small parts that older systems skip.

  • New shutoff valve and flexible connectors.
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe.
  • Expansion tank where the system calls for it.
  • Drain pan with a drain where a leak would damage finished space.
  • Gas leak test and vent check.
  • Hot water temperature set and explained.

Tank, tankless or hybrid

A tank heater is the lowest upfront cost. A tankless unit saves space and delivers hot water continuously, but needs a larger gas line or a heavier electrical circuit and a descaling schedule. A hybrid heat pump unit cuts electric use but needs space and airflow. We explain the plumbing and electric changes before you choose, and we do not recommend an upgrade that your building cannot support.

Installing in Secaucus

A lot of Secaucus housing is multi-unit, so an installation often starts with a call to the property manager. Ask about contractor rules, insurance requirements for work in the building and whether a shutoff is needed for the riser. In Harmon Cove, which was developed in the 1970s, buildings have central risers and shared walls, which affects when water can be shut off.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department whether your installation needs a permit. The department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue. The water your heater sees affects its life. Ask your water utility for its annual water quality report, or test your tap.

Code and permits for a new installation

A new water heater location, a new gas line or a changed vent path is a bigger job than a like-for-like swap, and it is covered by the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department before anything is cut. We tell you what we expect on the phone.
