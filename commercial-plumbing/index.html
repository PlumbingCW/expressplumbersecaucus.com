When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

Call at once for:

  • A floor drain or restroom backing up with wastewater.
  • A burst pipe or active leak in a ceiling.
  • No hot water in a kitchen or restroom.
  • A grease line overflowing.
  • Low pressure across the whole building.

Drains, grease and backups

Commercial drains handle more load than homes. Floor drains and grease lines clog and overflow. A cable machine clears most stoppages. Hydro jetting cleans grease buildup and can be scheduled to prevent repeat emergencies. If a camera is needed to find a break, see sewer inspection.

Leak detection in larger buildings

Commercial leak calls often take longer to resolve, because many systems share one building and tenants may share risers. We start with the meter test and zone isolation, then use listening gear and cameras. Be prepared for a slower, more methodical process than a house. See leak detection for methods.

Hot water in kitchens and offices

Restaurants and larger offices depend on water heaters that run harder than a home unit. Failed elements, scaled tanks and tripped controls are common. A kitchen without hot water cannot pass a health inspection in most places, so these calls get priority. See water heater repair for the diagnostic steps and replacement if a tank has failed.

Backflow testing and grease traps

Many commercial properties have a backflow preventer on the water line, and some must be tested on a schedule set by the water utility or the town. Testing is done by a tester certified for the assembly, so tell us if a test notice has arrived and we will coordinate repair of a failed device.

Grease traps are pumped by licensed haulers. We clear the drain lines that feed the trap and the line beyond it, and we can advise how often a line needs cleaning once we see what comes out.

Code, permits and landlord approval

Commercial work is held to the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and a tenant usually needs the landlord's written approval before pipes are cut. Permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department. Grease traps, backflow devices and restaurant drains may carry additional rules from the sewer utility.
