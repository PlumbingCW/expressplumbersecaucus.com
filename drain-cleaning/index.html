When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most single-sink clogs can wait for a same-day visit. A few signs mean the problem is bigger than one fixture and should not wait for morning.

  • Water or sewage comes up in a tub, shower or floor drain when you flush a toilet or run the washer.
  • More than one fixture is slow or gurgling at the same time.
  • A sewage smell rises from a basement drain.
  • Standing water in a kitchen sink will not move at all, and the dishwasher is full.

Shut off the washer and dishwasher, keep children and pets out of the affected room, and do not flush. Chemical openers are the one thing to leave out. They sit in standing water, burn skin if the water splashes, and make the clog harder to work on safely. Our after-hours crews carry cable machines for sinks and mains, so a night call does not mean waiting for a second truck.

Where the clog sits depends on which drains misbehave

The pattern tells us where to start. One slow fixture usually means a clog in its own trap or branch line. Several fixtures on the same floor point to a shared branch. A basement floor drain that bubbles when upstairs water runs points to the main line or the lateral that leaves the house.

One sink or tub

Hair, soap scum or a dropped object in the trap or short branch. A small cable usually clears it in one visit.

Kitchen sink and dishwasher

Grease and food solids pack the line past the garbage disposal. A kitchen machine with a cutting head clears it.

Toilet plus tub or floor drain

Water in the wrong place means the main is restricted. We run a larger cable, then look inside the pipe.

Whole house slow

A heavy blockage or root intrusion in the lateral. This is where camera work and jetting come in.

Kitchen sink clogs and what actually causes them

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Cooking fat cools inside the pipe, coats the wall, and catches rice, coffee grounds and egg shell until the opening is the size of a pencil. Running hot water after the fact only pushes the soft part further down.

Cutting the blockage out with a cable and a spiral head, then flushing the line, removes the coating instead of punching one hole through it. If a kitchen drain has been cleared twice in a year, the line probably has a low spot or a rough joint. A short camera look will tell you, and the sewer camera inspection page explains what we check.

How we clear it: cable, jet or camera first

A cable machine is the default for sinks, tubs and most main lines. It cuts through roots and soft blockages and costs less than other methods. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour grease and scale off the pipe wall, which suits repeat clogs and heavy buildup. Hydro jetting is not always the right first move on old, brittle pipe, which is why the camera sometimes comes first.

Slow tubs, shower drains and drain line leaks

Tubs and showers usually clog with hair and soap, and a small tool clears them without removing the fixture. A drain line that leaks under a floor shows up as a stain, a musty smell or a sink that clears and then drains slowly again. That is a repair, and the same camera work finds it. If you also see a rising water bill with no clog, look at the leak detection page.

Code and sewer rules for drain work

Drain piping inside a property follows the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and the town's sewer rules apply past the property line. In Secaucus, those rules come from the Secaucus Municipal Utilities Authority. If cleaning does not hold, a camera shows whether the pipe itself is the problem.
