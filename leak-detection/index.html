When a leak is an emergency

Slow leaks can wait for a scheduled visit. These cannot:

  • Water coming through a ceiling or running down a wall.
  • A hissing sound or a meter dial that spins fast with everything off.
  • Water near electrical panels, outlets or fixtures.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure along with wet floors or soft drywall.
  • Warm or wet patches on a floor above a slab.

Close the main shutoff, open a low faucet to drain the lines, and turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry. Then call.

Water meter still running or a high water bill

Many leak calls start here. A bill that doubles with no change in habits is a leak until shown otherwise. You can run a quick test yourself: shut off every tap and appliance, read the meter, wait two hours with nothing running, and read it again. If the number moved, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

  1. Read the meter

    Note the number and any small leak indicator dial.

  2. Water off, two hours

    No flushing, no ice maker, no irrigation.

  3. Read again

    Any movement means a leak.

  4. Shut the main

    If the dial stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, it is likely before the house valve.

Toilets are the usual cheap culprit, so check them first. See toilet repair for how.

Slab leaks and warm floors

A slab leak is a break in a pipe under or inside a concrete floor. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water at night, a damp carpet edge and a bill that climbs. We listen along the slab with electronic ground microphones and pinpoint the break before any concrete is opened.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks

Water shows up far from its source. A stain on a ceiling can come from a supply line, a drain, a bathroom above, a roof, or condensation. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where the wet area starts, so we know whether to look at plumbing or call a roofer.

Under-house, crawlspace and basement leaks

Leaks under a house or in a basement ceiling often drip for months and show only as mold smell or a floor that sags. We check supply lines, drain lines and the water heater area and confirm with the meter test.

Buried line, yard and meter box leaks

A leak between the meter and the house shows as a wet patch, a sinking spot in the yard or a meter that moves with the house off. Acoustic listening on the line finds it, and the water line repair page covers fixing it. For a leak at the meter box, the utility may own the part on the street side, so ask them before digging.

Irrigation, pools and commercial properties

Irrigation leaks hide under lawns and show as soggy zones. We check the zones and the backflow device. Pool leaks need a bucket test and pressure test and are limited to the plumbing, not the shell. Commercial leaks often involve more than one building system and a longer investigation. See commercial plumbing.

Hidden leaks in Secaucus

Secaucus has about 9,316 housing units, and a large share are in multi-unit buildings. A leak in a stacked building affects the unit below, so tell neighbors and the management office quickly, and ask whether the building has a shutoff for the riser.

Heavy rain also matters. During Ida on September 1, 2021, the town pumped out 91 basements, and groundwater and sewer backup were as likely as supply leaks that night. A basement with water after a storm is not always a plumbing leak, and we will tell you which it is.
