Emergency Water Line Repair in Secaucus, NJ

Emergency water line repair in Secaucus means fixing the pipe that carries water from the street to your house. When it leaks, you may see a wet or sinking spot in the yard, a spinning meter, or pressure that drops without warning. Dispatch is open 24/7.

If water is surfacing, close the main shutoff inside the house and call (551) 249-3045. If the leak is before the meter, the utility's valve is theirs to operate, so we tell you who to call.

Water line emergency or just low pressure

Pressure problems have several causes, so a quick check helps.

Pressure low everywhere

Meter valve, a partly closed main, a failing pressure reducing valve or a leak.

Low at one fixture

Clogged aerator, valve or local pipe. Not the service line.

Wet yard patch

Likely a leak on the service line between meter and house.

Meter moves with taps off

Water escaping after the meter. Start with a leak test.

Repair or replace the service line

A single leak on a sound pipe can be repaired with a section. Old galvanized, lead or heavily corroded lines often fail again, so replacement may cost less over time. Trenchless replacement pulls new pipe through the old path and avoids a long trench. Open-cut replacement is simpler and is used where access is easy or the old pipe has collapsed.

Is your service line lead?

Water utilities have been required to inventory lead service lines, so your utility may be able to tell you the material. A magnet and a scratch test at the entry point give a clue, but a licensed plumber should confirm. If the line is lead, replacement is the long-term fix. Who owns what

The utility generally owns the main and the meter. The line from the meter to the house is usually the owner's. Ask the utility where it draws the line for your address before work starts. Digging requires a markout call to 811 so buried utilities are located first, and we handle that step for our work.

Water lines in Secaucus

Secaucus sits in the Meadowlands, with ground that can be wet and soft near the Hackensack River. Soft ground can move and stress a buried line over time, and a leak near it may wash soil out slowly. A sinking patch on a lawn or a wet spot beside the driveway is a reason to test the line.

Your water bill names your water utility. Ask it for the annual water quality report and who to call for a leak at the meter. Permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department about line replacement. The department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue.

Lead service lines and New Jersey law

A 2021 New Jersey law, P.L. 2021, c.183, requires public water systems to inventory lead service lines and replace them within ten years. If your service line is lead, the utility has a duty to act, and your repair is the time to ask what is planned.

Pipe on your side of the shutoff follows the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Ask your water utility about the line, and about who owns which section, before any digging.
