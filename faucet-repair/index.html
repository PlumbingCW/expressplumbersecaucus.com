When a faucet or valve is an emergency

Call right away if:

  • Water will not stop even with the handle off.
  • A shutoff valve is spraying or has a snapped stem.
  • A sink supply line has burst or is weeping heavily.
  • Water is dripping from the ceiling below a sink or tub.
  • A shower valve leaks inside the wall.

Fixtures are the biggest group of calls we see. These are the usual:

Sink drains and traps

Repair or replace the faucet

A drip usually means a worn cartridge, seat or washer, and repair costs less than replacement. A faucet with a corroded body, cracked spout or discontinued parts is better replaced. We carry common cartridges and can match many brands, and when the brand is rare we tell you before ordering parts.

Old shutoff valves and sink leaks

Shutoff valves under older sinks often have not moved in years. Turning one may snap the stem or make it leak. We replace them with quarter-turn valves so the next emergency is easier. A leak under a sink can be a loose slip nut, a cracked trap or a failed supply line. Placing a dry paper towel under each joint finds the source in a few minutes. If water also drains slowly, see drain cleaning.

Fixtures in Secaucus homes

Many condos in Secaucus have older shutoff valves behind vanities. In a stacked building, a failed valve can stain a ceiling below, so replace one that seeps now rather than later. If water pressure at fixtures is low, the cause may be at the meter or the street, not the faucet. Your water bill names the utility to ask.

Water chemistry affects faucet life. Ask your water utility for its annual water quality report, or test your tap, to see why aerators scale up.

Flow limits and what a new faucet means

Federal rules cap the flow of new bathroom faucets at 2.2 gallons per minute, and WaterSense-labeled faucets use 1.5 gallons per minute or less. A new faucet saves water and money, and replacement piping follows the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15.
