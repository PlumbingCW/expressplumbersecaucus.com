Most installation work falls into a few types.

Fixtures

Sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers, laundry hookups.

New lines

Appliance connections

Basement baths

Rough-in, ejector pumps and venting.

Rough-in, then finish

Installation has two stages. Rough-in puts pipes in the walls and floor before they are closed. Finish sets fixtures and trim after walls are done. Inspections, where required, usually come after rough-in, so schedule the walls after the sign-off.

  1. Plan

  2. Rough-in

  3. Inspection

    If your project requires one.

  4. Finish

    Fixtures set, connections tested.

Basement bathrooms and ejector pumps

A basement bathroom below the sewer line needs an ejector pump to lift waste up. It requires venting, a sealed pit and a check valve. If your basement has had water, see basement waterproofing first.

Fixture choice affects plumbing

A freestanding tub, a double vanity, a rain shower head or a tankless water heater each change pipe sizes or locations. Decide fixtures before rough-in, not after. For heaters, see water heater installation.

Laundry hookups and appliances

Washer boxes, laundry sinks, ice makers and dishwashers each need the right supply, drain and shutoff. A washer standpipe that is too short or too low can back up. We install the box, the supply valves and the drain at proper height, and we recommend braided supply lines with accessible shutoffs.

Gas lines: safety first

Gas appliances and gas piping are different from water work. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before calling anyone else. We do not promise any response time for gas leaks. For planned work, such as moving a range or adding a dryer line, we tell you what is needed and what permit rules may apply.

Working in older buildings

Older Secaucus buildings may have cast iron drain stacks and mixed pipe materials. Cutting into them means matching materials and using proper couplings. We check the stack before adding a fixture that will load it, and we say so when a section of old cast iron should be replaced instead of tied into.

Renovations in Secaucus

Permit rules vary, so ask the Secaucus construction department what your project needs. The department is at the Town Hall Annex, 20 Centre Avenue, and its hours are listed on the town website. Plumbing in New Jersey is performed by a master plumber or authorized plumbing contractor, or by employees of one, according to the state's plumbing board. Ask any plumber for their license details and check them with the board.

In condos and townhouses, add your building's rules to the plan. Many require approval, insured contractors and limited work hours.

Which rules apply to new plumbing work

New fixtures, new supply lines and new drains follow the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts as its plumbing subcode in N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. New Jersey requires plumbing to be done by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or their employees, as the state's Division of Consumer Affairs explains. Ask any contractor to show you what they hold before work starts.
